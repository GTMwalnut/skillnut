# Module 2 - Partner and affiliate program design

Load this for Module 2. Build the parts in this order: benchmark, involvement ladder, partner types, attribution, commission mechanics, terms and prohibited activities, application and vetting, directory, asset kit. Everything is a draft until the user signs off the money and says go on publishing.

## D1. Benchmark, don't reinvent

Ask which vendor programs the user admires (or that their buyers already use) and read a few of their public partner, affiliate or expert pages. Copy the structure that works; never copy their numbers as if they were the user's. Record per program: tiers or levels, what moves a partner up, how attribution works, how and when payouts happen, what is prohibited, what the partner gets besides money.

A pattern seen in real vendor programs: status levels that rise with proof.

| Level | What the partner has shown | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| Learning | Signed up, learning the product | Free trial or plan, training, community, a referral link or code |
| Working with customers | Using it with clients or prospects | Feedback from the partner team on positioning and packages, co-selling |
| Proven | Referenceable customers | Official partner or expert status, directory listing, deal flow, a shared channel with the team, marketing support |

## D2. The involvement ladder (revenue share)

Pay more for more work. Three rungs:

| Rung | What the partner does | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Refer | Sends a lead or signup; your team does the rest | Lowest |
| Influence | Introduces, joins calls, helps the deal along | Middle |
| Self-close | Runs the demo and closes the deal themselves | Highest |

Illustrative only, for showing the shape: refer 5 percent, influence 15 percent, self-close 25 percent of qualified revenue (illustrative). The real rates are set by the user and signed off by whoever owns the money. Also set, with the user:
- **Qualified revenue**: what the share is calculated on. A common rule is undiscounted, billable revenue actually received; one-time fees, refunds and taxes excluded.
- **Duration**: for how many months after signup the share is paid.
- **Who decides the rung**: agree it per referral, in writing (email is fine), before the deal closes.

## D3. Partner types

Agree the types up front; they drive tiers, the directory and recruitment. Typical: specialist agencies in the product's field, boutique agencies, freelancers and independent consultants, sources of authority (people whose word carries weight with buyers), and implementation partners. Competitors are not partners.

## D4. Attribution rules (write them so nobody argues later)

- **Code or link.** Decide whether referrals are tracked by a code entered at signup, a tracked link, or both. If by code, the partner is responsible for making sure the code is entered.
- **No retroactive adds.** A code or link cannot be added after the account exists. Say it in the terms and in the partner kit.
- **Attribution window.** The user sets how long a click or referral stays credited. Tell partners to send their link at first touch: in a real case a vendor could not credit a referral because the client signed up without the link and the referral was older than the vendor's window.
- **Genuine first-time customers only.** An anti-fraud check confirms the referred account is new and real.
- **Self-referral is fraud.** That includes accounts under the same economic umbrella: family, cofounders, business partners. Account manipulation means disqualification, forfeiture of unpaid commission and clawback of paid commission.

## D5. Commission mechanics

- Calculated within a few days of month end, from revenue actually received.
- Paid to the payout email or account on file; business addresses only.
- A minimum payout threshold set by the user. Below it, a balance can roll over, or be redeemed as platform credit (some programs add a bonus for taking credit). Payout on request once the threshold is reached.
- The signed agreement, the referral log and the monthly pay-or-invoice cycle: hand off to the Agency Back Office skill, Module 5 (Referral agreement). No commission is paid by this skill; paying is a live action that needs its own go.

## D6. Terms and prohibited activities checklist

Draft the terms in these sections (structure taken from a real affiliate policy; wording is the user's):

| Section | What it covers |
|---|---|
| 1 Commission structure | Rungs, qualified revenue, duration |
| 2 Referral attribution | Code or link, responsibility, no retroactive adds, window, first-time check, self-referral and manipulation |
| 3 Tracking and payout | Calculation timing, payout destination, threshold, credit option |
| 4 Approval and termination | How partners are approved; the company may remove a partner at any time and may withhold the reason to protect fraud detection |
| 5 Promotion and conduct | Ethical and lawful promotion only; the prohibited list below |
| 6 Brand and content usage | Official assets only, never modified |
| 7 Eligibility | Business email; independent entities; no circumventing the rules; the company may apply eligibility rules retroactively |

Prohibited activities (tick each into the terms):
- [ ] Posing as the company's staff or as the company
- [ ] Bidding on the company's brand or trademark keywords in paid search
- [ ] Buying lookalike domains of the brand, including for cold email
- [ ] Spam, unsolicited bulk messages, or misleading content
- [ ] False or unapproved claims about the product
- [ ] Misusing or modifying brand assets
- [ ] Deceptive content of any kind (fake reviews, undisclosed incentives)

Add a line that the terms are a working draft for the user's lawyer.

## D7. Application and short vetting

- An application form published on the website, linked from LinkedIn. Fields: name, company, website, partner type, who their clients are, how they would use or recommend the product, a link to their work, business email.
- A short vetting step before approval. The owner checks: is this a real business with a working website; does their work match the partner types; any competitor or ownership conflict; any sign of the prohibited activities. Verdict Approve / Ask a question / Decline, with a one-line reason.
- Every applicant ends in exactly one state: approved, declined, or pending. Approving and declining are live actions; the owner does them or says go.

## D8. Directory

- A partner directory page on the website, plus a LinkedIn post or page section announcing partners. Each listing: name, what they specialise in, where clients can find them, and their level.
- Draft the page copy and one listing template. Publishing the page and each new listing needs a go.

## D9. Partner kit

What every approved partner receives: product access (free plan or credits), the brand assets, a short brief (who the product is for, approved claims, what not to say), a demo or onboarding session, their link or code with the attribution rules in plain words, and how to submit a referral.
