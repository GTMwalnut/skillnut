# Voice profile (loaded on demand)

A voice profile is a short style guide built from how the author actually talks, so every post sounds like them and not like LinkedIn. The best raw material is not their old posts. It is their meeting transcripts, because on a call nobody is performing for the feed.

## Inputs

- **Best:** about six call transcripts where the author explains something: a sales call, a client update, a mentoring chat, a discovery call. Pick different kinds of call, so the profile catches the voice and not one situation.
- **Good:** eight to ten past posts the author is proud of (not ghostwritten ones).
- **Also useful:** any standing feedback the author has given on drafts ("never close with a question", "don't use the word 'uncomfortable'").

Status-only calls ("we sent 400 emails this week") teach little. Calls where the author explains, argues or teaches are the ones to use.

## Method

1. **Pull about 25 passages** where the author explains or makes a point. Only their own lines; never quote a client or a guest, and strip client names and numbers from anything kept.
2. **Read across the passages for patterns** in six areas:

| Area | What to look for | Example of a finding (invented) |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence rhythm | Long builds then a short landing line? Short and clipped? Average length? | "Builds with 'and then', lands on a 2 to 4 word line. Aim for 8 to 18 words, split anything past 25" |
| Openers | The words they start explanations with | "Starts with 'So' or 'Basically'. Use once or twice a post, not every line" |
| Argument moves | How they build a case, in the order they use them | "1. Announce the count. 2. A concrete example from their own work. 3. A 'let's say' hypothetical. 4. Reflect the other view back. 5. The second-order consequence, then a recommendation" |
| Lexicon | Grounding phrases, softeners, transitions, favourite words | "Real numbers always, with 'roughly' and 'close to'. Softens with 'most likely'. Never says 'leverage'" |
| Tone and register | Consultative or punchy, formal or casual, how they disagree | "Teaches, never pitches. Disagrees softly: 'that might not be the right proxy'" |
| Spoken to written | What to keep from speech and what to cut | "Keep the numbered walk and the real numbers. Cut 'you know', stacked 'yeah yeah', and run-ons" |

3. **Keep only cross-call patterns.** A habit that shows up in one call is a mood. A habit that shows up in most of them is the voice. Write down what you cut and why (transcription errors, filler, one-off moods), so the author can put anything back.
4. **Write a do and don't table** of six to eight rows.
5. **Write one short worked example** in the voice: a post the author could plausibly have written, built only on details they gave you.
6. **Add the author's standing rules** from their feedback, quoted. These override the skill's defaults for this author. For example, if the author never closes on a question, the close comes from the "reach" or "authority" rows of the close table, never the "comments" row.

## Where the profile and the skill's rules meet

- **The profile wins on voice:** casing, rhythm, openers, favourite words, how they close.
- **The skill wins on honesty and craft:** never invent, no long dashes, no keyword-comment gates, no engagement pods. A profile can never license a made-up number.
- **A real habit that is on the tell list** (for example, defining things by contrast, "not X, we do Y") stays only if it shows up across most of the calls, and at most once a post. Stacked, it reads as a model.

## Packaging

Deliver the profile as one markdown file the author owns (`Voice Profile - <name>.md`). It works three ways: as input to this skill's Write and Rewrite modules on every run, pasted into a custom style or project instructions in their AI tool, or handed to a ghostwriter.

## Model report (illustrative)

> Voice profile done, from six of your calls: two sales calls, two client updates, a mentoring chat and a team sync. I pulled 24 passages where you explain something. Five patterns show up in almost every call: you start with "So", you announce how many points you'll make, you ground everything in a real number, you check in with "correct me if I'm wrong", and you end on a soft recommendation rather than a hard ask. I cut the filler and two habits that only showed up once. Your earlier feedback says never close on a question, so that's now a standing rule.
