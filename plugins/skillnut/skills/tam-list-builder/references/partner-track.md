# Partner track: a TAM of partners, not buyers

Load this when Stage 2 set the **partner** track. The list is of companies that could refer, resell or implement the product, and the right person inside each. All numbers are illustrative.

Two shapes, often both in one build:

| Shape | What it is | Where the companies come from |
|---|---|---|
| Excluded-are-partners | The agencies, consultancies, service firms, resellers and independents that your **buyer** ICP excludes. They are not buyers, so they are partner candidates | The removed and excluded files from earlier buyer builds, plus keyword search |
| Partner persona in a vertical | A role inside a different kind of company that sits next to your buyers (for example the people at finance or service providers who work with your buyers every day) | Keyword and industry-code company search, then people search by persona |

The one rule that never bends: **competitors are not partners.** Screen them out before anything else is built.

---

## PT1. Inventory, and prove nothing changed
- List every lead file in the client's folders, and fingerprint each (a content hash) before you start; many will be duplicates by content. Re-hash at the end and say "every original is unchanged".
- All new work goes in its own folder. Once a version is shared with the client, never edit it in place: the next one is List 2 or v2.

## PT2. Merge
Read the exclusion and classification files from earlier builds plus any extra files the owner points at; use the big master exports only to look up descriptions. One row per person and one per company, with country normalised and a scope column (in scope, out, unknown). Reconcile the counts.

## PT3. Discover candidates
- **Cheapest first:** companies earlier builds already labelled agency, consultancy, service firm, vendor or out-of-scope. They are pre-sorted for you.
- **Vertical shape:** company search by keyword and by industry code (NAICS or similar). Expect industry-code searches to be far too broad, and keyword searches to bring in tiny templated shells (lead-gen "funding" sites, parked pages); a cheap classifier prescreen plus a read catches them.

## PT4. Classify from proof, not from labels
Read each company's own description or website (not the LinkedIn blurb) and record:

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| Evidence | explicit (the site says it does the partner-relevant work), indirect (implied), none |
| Partner verdict | Yes / Maybe / No |
| Type | the partner types you agreed (for example specialist agency, consultancy, research or services firm, vendor, independent) |
| Tier | A / B / C (PT6) |
| Confidence and one-line reason | always |

Website reads move a lot: in one build they moved about half the candidates from "unknown" to "explicit". Website fetching can need the owner's approval; unreadable sites are excluded, not guessed. Read the Yes partners individually; rule-tier only the long tail, and sample it (in one build about 1 in 5 rule-tiered Tier C companies turned out to be a different kind of business).

## PT5. Competitor and ownership screen
- Remove direct competitors and their agencies or subsidiaries. Flag partners owned by a holding group that also owns a competitor, and vendors that may sell competing software. Keep the flag in an internal tab, not in the shared file.
- Run the client's own competitor and exclusion list against the result; if you do not have it yet, say the screen is incomplete.

## PT6. Tiers
| Tier | Typical rule |
|---|---|
| A | Specialist firms whose core service line is exactly where your product fits |
| B | Broader firms with a relevant service line |
| C | Adjacent firms with an operations-only line; the long tail, rule-tiered and sampled |
| Independents | Solo consultants, in their own tab |

## PT7. People
- Up to 5 per partner company, by partner persona, in this order: owner or leadership, partnerships or business development, the head of the relevant service line, operations or innovation leads.
- For a vertical persona, score the role (for example a classifier role score of 0.5 or more for front-line roles) and drop support and back-office roles.
- **Before removing any people, show the owner the full list of names, not a sample, and wait for approval.**
- Solo consultants go in a separate tab. In the EU and UK a sole trader is a private individual under privacy and e-marketing law (GDPR, PECR): note the lawful-basis question before anyone emails them.

## PT8. Contacts and spend
Own files first (earlier exports often hold most of what you need). Then a contact waterfall with a per-company cap and a credit estimate shown before any credit is spent; the owner names the provider order and any provider cap. Partners with no contact go to their own internal tab, with the Tier A/B count called out.

## PT9. Suppression and identity
- CRM suppression: check deals, notes and activities, not just a status tag. Bulk-applied custom fields can say "Customer" on a company that has no deals at all. The owner sets the rule (for example "a won deal or a live conversation", or "the status field says Customer"), and you apply exactly that.
- Then the Campaign Send Checklist: prior outreach (the catalogue rule: replied ever out, positive reply takes the company out, touched in 100 days out), identity (stale emails at a former employer go LinkedIn only), clean, and email verification before launch.

## PT10. Partner copy
- Build it from the client's own brief, in their words, and get it approved; approved copy is then frozen and the owner edits in the sequencer.
- Open with an open-ended question about the partner's own work, never "quick question".
- The ask is about deals: "Do you have any deals you're pursuing that need a [partner type]?" and a short call "to explore a partnership".
- Spintax only on a few grammatical words, never whole sentences.

## The deliverable
A workbook with Summary, Partner Companies (verdict, type, tier, evidence, reason, flags), Contacts, and Independents. Internal tabs that do not go to the client: competitors and flags, screened out, needs a second look, partners with no contact. A README listing the steps, rules, limits (unreviewed companies, rule-tiered tails, unknown countries, partners without contacts) and the next phase.
