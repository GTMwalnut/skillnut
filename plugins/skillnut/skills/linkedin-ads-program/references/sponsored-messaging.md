# Sponsored messaging (message ads in the LinkedIn inbox)

A message from a real person at the client, delivered to the inbox instead of the feed, with a CTA button. Recipients can't reply. Delivery in the EU and EEA reaches only members who opted in to these ads; check the current rule before targeting there.

## 1. Choose the sender
- A founder, VP or director beats an account executive; seniority lifts opens and response.
- The sender's profile must be credible (photo, headline, recent activity).
- Confirm the sender is comfortable; people withdraw later. If they do: you can't remove an approved sender from the account, but approval only makes them selectable. Stop selecting them in every ad, and approve the replacement the same way.

## 2. Sender approval (each step needs the client to act; tell them exactly what to do)
1. **Connect.** The person managing the ad account sends a LinkedIn connection request to the sender (LinkedIn may ask for the sender's email to send it). Ask the client to tell the sender to accept it; if two people manage the account, send from both.
2. **Request.** Once connected, add the sender in Campaign Manager (account settings, sponsored messaging sender permissions) and send the request.
3. **Approve.** The sender approves from their own LinkedIn account. Direct link: `https://www.linkedin.com/campaignmanager/accounts?destination=sponsored-inmail-sender-permissions`. LinkedIn's help article on sender permissions explains the same steps.
4. Select the sender in each sponsored messaging ad.

## 3. Creative
- Banner 300 x 250 px. Keep it to the value proposition; the message copy and the CTA button drive the action, so the banner doesn't need "Book a demo".
- Remove any customer logo not cleared for use. Keep the banner wording consistent with the message positioning (if the copy plays a feature down, the banner shouldn't headline it). A/B test banner variants (for example feature-led versus platform-led) rather than arguing.

## 4. Copy
- Write as the sender: conversational, question-led, short (not a long sales email), from the operational pain the audience lives with.
- Angles to test: the workflow pain (manual reviews, version tracking, approvals over email); the platform with an optional feature layer; social proof (approved customer names only); broader modernisation; a peer-style question.
- Subject lines: test personalised ones with the First Name macro.
- Start with the 2 strongest variations; replace the weaker one with a new one once there's data.
- Copy and banners get written client approval before launch.

## 5. Audience
- Start warm: website visitors, report downloaders, case-study readers, form openers, remarketing audiences, and SQL or opportunity audiences where appropriate.
- Then test a cold audience as its own campaign; in a real program sponsored messaging produced demo requests mostly from cold audiences while sponsored content's came from remarketing. Test, don't assume.
- Apply the same exclusions as the rest of the program.

## 6. Cost and tracking
- Billed per send; plan for roughly US$0.40-0.50 per send (so 10,000 sends is about US$4,000-5,000). Open rates in the 50-60% range were typical in a real program.
- Recipients stay anonymous unless they act (click and submit, or book). Reporting is company-level.
- Tag sponsored messaging leads separately from sponsored content leads in the shared lead sheet; the CRM won't always show the difference.
- The CTA can link straight to the booking calendar.
