# Weekly ops and the weekly report

## 1. Every week, before writing the report
1. **Sync health:** CRM lead sync connected? Last sync date? (crm-and-tracking.md, section 1)
2. **Reconcile leads:** LinkedIn form submissions vs CRM contacts from LinkedIn vs the shared lead sheet. Explain every gap: duplicates (the same person submitting twice), leads stuck in a broken sync, existing contacts not appearing in a "paid social" list because their original source was set earlier. Say the reconcile line out loud.
3. **Alerts:** did every demo request trigger an alert? Any missed ones get sent manually and the trigger fixed.
4. **Lead quality:** each new lead marked ICP or not, with the reason; send borderline ones to the client for a yes or no before follow-up.
5. **Exclusions:** refresh customers, competitors and lifecycle lists if they're manual.
6. **Spend vs plan:** pacing per campaign; any campaign under-delivering at the minimum bid gets a small bid raise.

## 2. Report template (one doc, newest week on top; email a short highlights note with the link)

**Header:** client, reporting period, total spend, prepared by.

1. **Executive summary** - leads, spend, CPL, the week's headline in 3 to 4 sentences; the main win and the main problem.
2. **Overall campaign performance** - impressions, clicks, CTR, CPC, spend, leads, CPL.
3. **Engagement and brand awareness** - engagements, engagement rate, follower growth.
4. **Middle of funnel** - per content offer: leads, CPL, CTR, best ad.
5. **Bottom of funnel** - demo campaigns: sponsored content and sponsored messaging separately (sends, opens, open rate, clicks, demo requests, cost per demo request), then combined.
6. **Account-based marketing** - companies reached in the matched-account audience; companies that produced paid leads.
7. **Lead quality overview** - by title, seniority, company, region; share that fits the ICP.
8. **Pipeline quality** - leads that became SQLs, opportunities, meetings booked and held.
9. **Top performing ads** - creative, copy, metric.
10. **Campaign optimization log** - every change this week, one line each, with the reason (see below).
11. **Recommendations and next steps** - numbered, each with the why.
12. **Pending initiatives** - waiting on whom.

## 3. Optimization log rules
- One variable per test, stated: "Tested a Groups-based audience inside the case-study campaign"; "isolated one industry"; "added a 10,000+ employee size cut"; "refreshed the single-image retargeting audience"; "switched demo CTA to the booking calendar".
- Say what each test is meant to learn and when it will be judged.
- Pauses and budget moves are logged with the numbers that triggered them.

## 4. Decisions to make most weeks
- **Budget toward the real goal.** If meetings are the goal, shift budget from content to demo campaigns once content leads are steady, and accept a higher cost per lead on demo ads if they convert to meetings.
- **Keep the efficient engine running.** Don't kill the cheapest lead source to fund tests; trim it instead.
- **Don't judge a high-cost ad too early.** A case-study ad can cost several times a report ad per lead and still win on meetings; give it until there's downstream data.
- **Form drop-off.** Many people open a form and don't submit (90% drop-off was seen in a real program). Look at form length, headline and the offer before blaming the audience.
- **Demo drop-off.** People who request a demo but don't book: put the calendar on the thank-you screen, send the booking link instantly, follow up on LinkedIn and phone, not only email (security gateways can swallow follow-up email).
- **Geography.** Worldwide targeting lets cheaper markets absorb the daily budget before the priority markets wake up; split priority regions into their own campaigns.
- **Direct-to-calendar vs form then calendar.** The form captures the lead even if they don't book; direct-to-calendar removes a step but loses non-bookers. Run form-then-calendar first to set a baseline, then test direct-to-calendar as its own campaign.

## 5. Recurring recommendations worth checking
- Sync company-level lifecycle lists (SQL and Opportunity companies), not only contacts.
- Launch sponsored messaging for warm audiences once remarketing pools are big enough.
- Upload intent-data company lists (from an intent provider) as matched audiences for ABM campaigns.
- Set up the Revenue Attribution Report and review LinkedIn's influence on pipeline every month.
- Pull the 90-day company-level report against customer and open-deal lists.

## 6. Monthly
- Round review: what converts, what to scale, what to cut; next round's budget and projection with real numbers.
- One-page summary of every running and recently paused ad for stakeholders who can't navigate Campaign Manager: creative, full copy (intro, headline, CTA), landing page, campaign and audience in one line, performance notes, grouped by campaign.

## 7. Executive report (end of a round, for a stakeholder who won't open Campaign Manager)

A short PDF or doc, one page per idea, built from the campaign stats and the daily lead-form exports. Same reconciliation rules as the weekly report. Layout, in order:

1. **Header:** client, who it is prepared for and by, the ad account, the reporting window.
2. **Overall snapshot (tiles):** total spend, impressions, clicks, CTR, leads, how many of those leads fit the ICP, cost per lead, demos booked.
3. **Snapshot by offer:** one row per offer with its funnel stage (middle or bottom of funnel), spend, leads and cost per lead, plus a total row. Then one small table per offer: spend, impressions, clicks, CTR, leads, cost per lead. This is where the reader sees that a demo or case-study offer costs several times a content offer per lead, and why that can still be fine.
4. **Lead analysis:**
   - **Deduped leads:** leads received vs unique people (the same person submitting twice, or on two offers, counts once), cross-checked against the platform's own lead count, with the gap stated.
   - **Seniority mix:** a table of level, count and share (C-suite, head of, director, manager, specialist, and "other or unclear from title"), plus the single most common title.
   - **Companies:** unique companies, whether leads are spread or concentrated, and the recognisable accounts among them (client-facing only).
   - **Repeat engagers:** people who submitted more than once, and those who crossed between offers (for example a survey and then a demo). These are the warmest people in the program; name them only in the client's copy, never in anything shared outside.
   - **Data quality:** work vs personal email domains.
5. **Winners, one page per offer:** the landing page, the offer's numbers, the top copy variants verbatim with their leads, and the winning creative with its leads and cost per lead.
6. **Coming next:** new creative or formats in test with no data yet, labelled as such so nobody reads them as results.

Rules: every figure comes from the platform or the form exports for the stated window; leads are deduped before any count is shown; the repeat-engager table carries personal data and stays inside the client's systems.
