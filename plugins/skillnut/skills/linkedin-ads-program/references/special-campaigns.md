# Special campaign types

## 1. Promoting a webinar or event run by a partner
- **Brief to collect:** title, date and time with time zones, registration URL, speakers, abstract, 3 takeaways, audience (titles, seniority, industries, regions), flight window (start now, stop the morning of the event), budget, creative assets (partner badge, speaker headshots from the event page).
- **Partner rules first.** Educational sessions under a partner's non-promotion policy: your brand may appear, but no product names, no demo or "see how we solve this" language, no product screenshots. The ad sells the session and the research only.
- **Registration path.** If registration lives on the partner's platform:
  - LinkedIn can't track conversions there. Options: UTMs on the link plus the partner's referrer and `utm_medium` export (ask whether their platform keeps `utm_medium`; some delete it); or a LinkedIn lead gen form (one pre-filled field, trackable) that sends the join link by email, but only if the partner's system accepts it as a registration, otherwise people must fill two forms.
  - A landing page on your own domain that sends people to the partner form adds a step; use it only if you need the Insight Tag.
- **Budget.** Short flights; reallocate from running campaigns plus a top-up, approved in writing. Expect modest numbers with a short runway or a weekend in the middle.
- **Report:** spend, impressions, clicks to the registration page, and registrations attributed via UTMs.
- **Event air cover** (your own event presence): ads to the target list one week before and one week after the event. Respect the event's rules on attendee lists; don't target attendee or speaker lists you aren't clearly allowed to use.

## 2. Promoting a survey
- LinkedIn has no poll ad format. Use a Website Visits campaign (sponsored content or sponsored messaging) to the survey link. Sponsored messaging suits long surveys: people can come back to the message later.
- Lead gen forms allow only up to five custom questions, so they can't carry a full survey. Skip the form if the survey promises anonymity: the form email can't be tied to a completion.
- **Tracking:** a dedicated survey collector link for ads, with `utm_content` per campaign; or a completion redirect to your own thank-you page with the Insight Tag (only if the survey tool's plan allows the redirect).
- **Audience:** one audience per campaign (warm matched list; cold target list; sponsored messaging) so results compare.
- **Geography:** match the previous round's countries if the survey compares rounds; worldwide targeting lets cheap regions absorb the budget.
- **Exclusions:** customers are good respondents; include them. Keep competitors out.
- **Copy accuracy:** the survey's real name and round, real length in minutes, and exactly how any prize draw is entered.
- **Economics:** clicks are expensive and respondents aren't leads; compare against emailing your own opted-in list and partner shares before buying reach.

## 3. Case study ads with a customer who must stay anonymous
- Attribute the story generically ("a top [industry] company") and pair it with a strip of several approved customer logos framed as a sampling, so no single logo is tied to the story.
- Use only approved quotes and stats from the client's customer reference sheet.
- Copy pattern: lead with the hard result (for example "[N]X output, same team size"), then the mechanism, then the CTA. Test it against a problem-led version.
- Journey: ad > lead gen form > thank-you screen > PDF (skip the extra landing page). A delivery email with the PDF follows from the CRM.
