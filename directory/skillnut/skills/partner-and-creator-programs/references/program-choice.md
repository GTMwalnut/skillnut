# Module 1 - Program choice

Load this for Module 1. The output is a one-page program decision: which programs, core or foundation, who owns them, and what the company must supply.

## Why these programs exist (teach this)

- When a new AI feature or a new piece of software comes out, the first people who learn it are agencies and freelancers. Full-time employees learn it later.
- That creates a talent gap: companies want the new tool but have nobody inside who can set it up. Agencies, implementers and creators fill the gap, and the software company earns a real share of its revenue through them.
- So it makes sense to sell to and through agencies, partners and creators, not only to end buyers.
- Creators become your marketing agents whenever the company hits a milestone: funding, expansion, a new product release. A program built before the milestone is ready to amplify it.

## Program types

| Program | What the other side does | What they get | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referral or affiliate | Sends you a lead or a signup through a tracked link or code | A share of the revenue from that customer | Core: can produce revenue in the first months |
| Implementation partner | Influences the deal, or runs the demo and closes it, then sets the product up for the client | A larger share, a directory listing, deal flow | Core |
| Creator | Posts genuine content about the product on LinkedIn, YouTube, X or Reddit | Free plan or credits, community, learning resources, early access, rewards | Foundation: compounds slowly |
| Hybrid creator-affiliate | A creator who also carries a tracked link | Creator perks plus a commission; their audience gets a better offer through the link | Foundation that can earn |
| Publication partner | A publication covers or cites the product | A useful story, data or expert view for their audience | Foundation: slow, high leverage for AI search |

Rule of thumb from the real plan this is built on: the partner or affiliate program sits beside outbound as a core revenue driver for the first 90 days; creators and publications are foundation work that compounds and should start small.

## Who partners and creators are

- Partners: specialists in the field your product serves, boutique agencies, freelancers, and "sources of authority" (people whose recommendation carries weight with your buyers). Quality over volume.
- Creators: GTM engineers (full time or freelance), agency owners, and creators in B2B marketing who use AI. Adjust the list to the product's buyers.
- Competitors are never partners.

## Ownership

- One person owns the programs: a program manager or community manager. It can be part time, or a shared responsibility inside marketing (one real plan had the community manager double as a marketing associate).
- If an agency or consultant sets the program up, the consultant builds the foundation and coaches the owner directly, so the company can run it without them.
- Write the owner's weekly tasks into the decision: approve applications, review creator drafts, send the monthly brief, update the report.

## What the company must supply (list it in the decision)

| Need | Why |
|---|---|
| Product access or free credits to issue | Partners and creators must use the product to recommend it |
| Brand assets (logo, banners) | So partners and creators use official assets, unmodified |
| One point of contact | So applications and drafts don't sit unanswered |
| Sign-off on the revenue share | The rates are a money decision, not a marketing one |

## Output: program decision (one page)

| Program | Core or foundation | Why for this product | Owner | Start month | First pilot size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then the supply list above with a named supplier per row, and the open decisions (rates, attribution window, payout threshold, creator floors, posting minimum) with "owner to set".
