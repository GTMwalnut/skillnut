# Module 4 - Creator program

Load this for Module 4. A creator program is a near-zero-cost content engine: creators get free product access and perks; in return they post quality content, steered by what the company cares about most right now. Build in this order: creator profile, sourcing, vetting, offer, guidelines, onboarding, monthly brief, review and moderation, engagement. Nothing is sent, issued or published without a go.

## C1. Creator profile

Who the ideal creator is, written for this product. A starting set for a B2B GTM or AI tool:
- GTM engineers, full time or freelance
- Agency owners
- Creators in the B2B marketing space who use AI

Plus, for each: the platform (LinkedIn, YouTube, X, Reddit), the topics they must already cover, and the audience floor the user sets per platform.

## C2. Sourcing

- Actively watch who is posting about the category on LinkedIn and YouTube; keep a running sheet.
- Add people who already mention or use the product, customers who post, and partners from Module 3 who also create.
- Personally reach out and ask if they would like to be a creator. Each invitation is a send and needs a go; you draft them.

## C3. Vetting (by hand)

Lists and AI can count followers; they cannot reliably judge whether posts are good or whether someone posts consistently. The owner looks.

| Column | What to record |
|---|---|
| Platform and link | |
| Audience | Followers or subscribers, against the floor the user set per platform (for example a few thousand LinkedIn followers, a smaller floor on YouTube, illustrative) |
| Covers the topic | Yes / partly / no, with one example post |
| Quality | The owner's read: original, useful, shows real work |
| Posting frequency | Roughly how often, over recent months |
| Uses or would use the product | Yes / no / unknown |
| Conflicts | Promotes a competitor heavily, misleading claims |
| Verdict | Invite / Watch / No, with a one-line reason |

Every candidate lands in exactly one verdict; print the reconcile line. Pilot: invite a handful first.

## C4. The offer

What creators get (pick what the company can give):
1. A free plan, demo account or credits, so they can create real posts and videos, and use it in their own business
2. Access to a community (a private channel or group)
3. Learning resources (videos, guides)
4. Beta access to new features
5. A points-and-rewards system for posting (goodies, more credits)

Optional, for a hybrid creator-affiliate program: a tracked creator link with a commission, a special offer for the creator's audience through that link, a badge for their profile, and a profile on the company's partner page.

Each creator gets a demo and onboarding. Issuing a free plan or credits is a live action: list who gets what, and wait for the go.

## C5. Guidelines (the creator agrees to these)

Draft them as a short page; the wording is the user's:
- **Active product user.** Content comes from genuine use of the product, not just the perks.
- **Staying active.** At least the minimum number of dedicated pieces per quarter the user sets; tell us if you need a break.
- **Comfortable creating content.** Not the right fit if you aren't.
- **Freedom with quality.** Post what is relevant to your audience; we value content that shows what the product can do and how your team uses it.
- **Original and authentic.** Reusing someone else's content goes against the program.
- **Personal over promotional.** Lead with the story or the insight; the product comes second.
- **Dedicated, not a roundup.** The post is about this product; no tool comparisons or roundups naming other tools.
- **Soft calls to action.** Curiosity over hard sell ("give it a spin", "DM me for credits").
- **Plain URL, not an @mention.** Write the product's web address as plain text in the post rather than tagging the company page.
- **Drafts 48 hours ahead.** Share each draft at least 48 hours before posting, for a light review.
- **Open communication.** Mutual trust; feedback both ways.
- **Quality community.** Membership is mutual opt-in; the company may end it at any time.

## C6. Monthly brief

Sent each month, themed to what matters most to the company now (a launch, a new feature, a funding round, an expansion). Sections:

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Key dates | Ship date, draft due, go-live, the event the call to action points at |
| The goal | One sentence. For a launch, two phases: before it ships, anticipation; after it ships, real experience |
| Step 0 | Try the product first and capture one moment that surprised you; that moment is the post |
| Angles | A few concrete angles to choose from; the creator picks one and doesn't mix |
| Audience | Who the post is for and what they care about |
| Facts you can use | Approved claims only, each one checkable |
| What you can say / what still applies | Allowed lines for this phase; the standing rules |
| Calls to action | The tracked link or event, the free-account invite, soft lines |
| Submission | Draft due date, where to send it, the 48-hour rule |

## C7. Review and moderation

- Light and manual: a few minutes per draft, by the owner. Check against the guidelines: one angle, approved facts only, soft call to action, plain URL, no roundup, original. Return specific notes, not a rewrite. The **LinkedIn Post Generator** skill can diagnose a draft.
- Track each post: due, draft received, reviewed, posted, link.
- A creator who repeatedly misses the minimum or breaks the guidelines gets a conversation first; removal is a live action with its own go.

## C8. Engagement

- A roadmap session for creators, monthly (or quarterly to start), where the product team shares what's new and coming, and rallies creators around the next launch.
- Before each milestone (funding, launch, expansion), brief the creators early so they post in the same week.

## Output

`Creator program/`: creator profile, sourcing and vetting sheet, offer, guidelines page, onboarding flow, first monthly brief, post tracker.
