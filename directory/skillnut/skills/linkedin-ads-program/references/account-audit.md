# Account audit: 15 points

Run on any account with history. Read-only. For each point: what to look for, why it matters, the fix. Report worst first. Start with lifetime spend, number of campaigns and groups, and objectives in use.

| # | Check | What to look for | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Launch day | New campaigns or ads started on Thursday to Sunday | LinkedIn shows a new ad to a test slice and scores it on early click-through rate (CTR). End-of-week CTR is low, so the ad gets a poor quality score and rarely recovers | Launch on Monday or Tuesday. If that week has holidays, move the launch a week earlier or later. Only break this for time-bound campaigns |
| 2 | Naming and structure | Campaigns named after one-off things (an event, a year, a funding round); campaign groups organised ad hoc | A campaign builds a relevance history. New ads in an old, good campaign start with that credit; a brand-new campaign starts from zero and pays more per click for days | Name campaigns after what never changes: `Format - Objective - Targeting` (for example `Single Image - Website Visits - IT Director - 50+ employees - Banking - US`). Keep them running and swap ads inside them. Three campaign groups: Top of funnel (awareness), Middle (lead generation), Bottom (demo, trial, call) |
| 3 | Objectives | Brand Awareness, Video Views, Website Conversions, Job Applicants in use | Awareness only allows automated or CPM bidding; Video Views bills per 2-second view; LinkedIn's conversion optimisation has less data than other platforms; job objectives limit formats and bids | Use Website Visits (pay only for visits), Lead Generation (in-platform forms; volume) and Engagement (cheaper when under about 35% of clicks are social actions). Video can run as a creative under Website Visits or Lead Gen on cost-per-click; only run a Video Views campaign once engagement is proven high |
| 4 | Audience Expansion and Audience Network | Either box ticked in any campaign | Expansion lets LinkedIn add "similar" people; the Audience Network serves on partner sites where much of the traffic is bots. Both dilute the precise targeting you pay a premium for | Untick both, everywhere |
| 5 | Targeting | Company lists with no further filter, very large audiences, lookalikes, one campaign mixing every audience | You can't see which audience works, and you pay to reach people who can't buy | Within each industry and size band, split into separate campaigns: Job Title; Job Function + Seniority; Skills + Seniority; Groups + Seniority; plus Remarketing, Matched Accounts (layered with function, title or skills) and Matched Contacts. Add a seniority layer to each |
| 6 | Bidding | Maximum Delivery regardless of CTR; accepting LinkedIn's suggested bid | You hand LinkedIn the controls and pay what others pay | Start on Manual CPC with "enhanced" unticked, at the minimum bid to run. Raise slightly only if you aren't getting impressions or spending the daily budget. When a campaign's CTR is above 1%, switch to a CPM bid (bid high, not low, or CTR collapses). After 4 to 5 days, if the audience saturates and CTR falls below 1%, go back to Manual CPC |
| 7 | Ad copy | Long text-heavy intros, weak hooks, feature-led copy, weak CTAs | People scroll fast; anything after about 150 characters hides behind "See more" | Pain point, then business impact, then solution, then one clear CTA. Intro under 150 characters (ideally under 135); headline under 60. Contrasting colours (warm accents stand out against LinkedIn's blue, grey and white) if brand rules allow |
| 8 | Testing method | Several elements changed between ad versions | You can't tell what moved the result | Single-variable tests in this order: intro copy, image, headline, CTA, lead form. Start with intro vs intro (pain vs aspiration, fear vs gain, statistic vs curiosity) |
| 9 | Insight Tag | Partner ID on the website differs from Campaign Manager's | Website retargeting audiences stay empty, conversions don't count | Fix before launching anything new; re-verify that the website-visitor audience starts filling |
| 10 | Account-based targeting | Company lists used alone | Ads reach everyone at the company, including people who will never buy | Layer company lists with titles, functions, seniority or skills |
| 11 | Remarketing | No video-viewer, image-engager, form-opener or website-visitor campaigns | The warmest people get no follow-up ad | Build dedicated remarketing for website visitors, product-page visitors, lead form openers, video viewers, single-image engagers, company page followers and CRM lists. Promote demos, consultations, customer stories and case studies to them |
| 12 | Lead forms | Asking for a general email; vague form headline | Personal emails and weaker leads | Ask for work email. Strengthen the form headline and say why to submit. Keep forms short; fields LinkedIn pre-fills cost nothing |
| 13 | Creative consistency | Graphic says "Read", headline says "Download", form says something else | Mixed messages lower conversion | One CTA wording across graphic, intro, headline, form and thank-you page |
| 14 | Location | "Recent or permanent location" | You pay for travellers | "Permanent location" |
| 15 | Lookalikes | Lookalike audiences in use | LinkedIn optimises for volume; you need cost efficiency. Lookalikes give away the targeting you pay for | Remove; keep targeting in your own hands |

Close the audit with priorities in this order: fix the Insight Tag; restructure into evergreen funnel-stage campaigns; split audiences by targeting method; rewrite copy pain-first; build remarketing; adopt single-variable testing; prove performance in a first round before scaling.
