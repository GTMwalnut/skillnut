# Founder readiness check: hire, agency, or wait

Load this in Mode C when the person asking is a founder (or a founder-side operator) deciding whether to invest in an outbound engine at all. The same gates work from the agency side as a go/no-go before taking a client: take it, offer a pilot, or decline with reasons.

The point of the check: a founder who builds an outbound engine too early burns money and then concludes "outbound does not work for us". The honest answer is often "not yet, and here is what to do first". That answer is worth more to them than a plan.

Every number in the examples below is illustrative unless the founder gave it to you.

## 1. Intake (ask for all of it, in one message)

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Paying customers today, and monthly revenue | Proof that someone buys, and how much budget is real |
| Price point or ACV, and typical customer lifetime | Decides whether outbound can pay back at all |
| Testimonials or case studies the founder can show publicly | Cold outreach borrows trust from them |
| How well they know their TAM (how many companies and people, which segments) | An engine needs a list it can rotate through for months |
| What they can spend a month on tools and data, before any people cost | Below a floor, the engine cannot run |
| What already works (founder posts, referrals, inbound, partners) | The cheaper channels come first |

## 2. The four gates

Run them in order. A failed gate does not end the conversation; it changes the recommendation.

| Gate | Pass | What a fail means |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Unit economics | Monthly cost of the engine divided by what one customer is worth gives a number of new customers a month the founder believes they can win | The product is priced too low for paid outbound at this stage. Lean on founder content, partners and product-led loops |
| 2. Proof | At least a handful of paying customers (10 to 20 is a good sign) and testimonials on the website | Outreach has nothing to borrow trust from. Get the first customers and their quotes up first |
| 3. TAM | They can name the first list: for example 15,000 to 20,000 people in the core segment | Build the list before the engine. Months of sending need a universe that can be rotated without re-hitting the same people inside the 100-day cooldown |
| 4. Budget | They can fund tools, data and infrastructure every month, on top of any people cost | A founder who cannot fund the tools will not see results from anyone. Say so kindly |

**The break-even line, in the founder's own numbers.** Example (illustrative): a GTM engineer at $3,000 a month plus $600 of tools, for a product at $150 a month, needs 24 new paying sign-ups every month just to cover the engine. Write the sum out, then ask: "Is 24 new customers a month from cold outreach realistic for you today?" Usually the founder answers the readiness question themselves.

## 3. The channel order for an early-stage founder

Before an outbound engine, in this order:

1. **Founder-led content.** The founder (and ideally the team) posts often on LinkedIn. Boost the posts that land, then identify the people who engage and email them. This is warmer than any cold list.
2. **Partners and affiliates.** For low-priced products: agencies, freelancers and creators who sell or post for a commission or for free product. A simple tier (for example 10 percent for a referral, more for a closed deal) is enough to start.
3. **Outbound infrastructure.** Domains, inboxes, a sequencer, LinkedIn automation, run on a rotated TAM. Cold emails carry no links in the first touch; offer the insight in plain text and send the link when they reply.
4. **Paid ads last.** Ads burn budget fast with no proof to point at. They come once there are enough paying customers (several dozen) to build audiences and creative from.

## 4. The verdict

Give one answer, with the gate results as the reason.

| Verdict | When | What to tell them |
|---|---|---|
| **Wait** | Gate 1 or 2 fails | What to do for the next 90 days instead: the channel order above, a target number of paying customers and testimonials, and the TAM list built. Offer to revisit when they hit it |
| **Agency or pilot** | Gates pass, but there is no one in-house to own outbound, or the motion is unproven | A 90-day pilot that builds the engine and proves the message. Then either the engine is handed to someone on their team, or it stays on retainer. Write the hand-over as a normal, good outcome |
| **Hire** | Gates pass, the motion is proven, and the volume justifies a full-time owner | Hire a GTM engineer to run it. Point at what they need on day one: the TAM, the infrastructure, the messaging that already works. An agency can build the engine first and hand it to the hire |

As a rule of thumb from real founder calls: hiring a full-time GTM engineer, or starting paid ads, usually makes sense only after the company has around 50 paying customers. Before that, the founder's time and the cheaper channels carry more.

## 5. Agency side: the same gates as a go/no-go

Before you take a client, run the four gates on them.

| Result | Move |
|---|---|
| All pass | Take it, in the shape Decision 0 picks |
| Proof or TAM is thin | Offer a small paid pilot with a clear success bar, not a long retainer |
| Unit economics or budget fails | Decline, or offer the readiness check itself as the deliverable. A client who will not fund the tools will blame the agency for the results |

## 6. What the founder gets

A one-page readiness note, not a plan: the intake as they gave it, the four gates with pass or fail and the reason, the break-even sum, the verdict, and the next 90 days in five or six lines. If the verdict is "agency or pilot" or "hire", offer to write the plan next (Mode A).
