# Webinar demand: promote, follow up, repurpose (Option F)

Load this when the user picks Option F. It runs the demand cycle around one webinar, or a recurring community session: promotion before, two follow-up tracks after (people who attended, and people who registered but did not show), a pre-send check, and the content that outlives the hour.

## Before the webinar: promotion

| Lever | How |
|---|---|
| Staff amplification | One executive post, boosted through the company's ad account, then 7 or 8 team members reshare or post their own take over three days. Four or five people posting is enough to matter |
| Warm pipeline | Reps email their stalled and early-stage deals (for example people met at earlier conferences) with a personal invite |
| Newsletter | Only to people who opted in (see below) |
| Engagement | Ask the panelists for one or two poll questions ahead of time |
| Registration page | Share the link with the team in one place so everyone uses the same one |

### Newsletter invites: three options, three risk levels

| Option | How | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Consent first | A short plain email from the sending tool asks if they want the newsletter; only people who say yes go into the newsletter tool (Beehiiv, Mailchimp or similar) | Lowest. Slow, but protected under CAN-SPAM and similar rules |
| 2. Plain-text segmented | Plain-text newsletter to a cold list, segmented by interest | Moderate spam and compliance risk; fast test of interest |
| 3. Designed newsletter | A visual newsletter to warmer contacts | Highest compliance risk unless everyone opted in. Keep it to people you already interact with |

Default to option 1 and say why.

## After the webinar

### 1. Get the attendee report

Ask the host platform or the partner organiser when the attendee report arrives. Split it into **attended** and **registered only**. Remove your own staff.

### 2. Enrich and run a pre-send check

Run the standard pre-send checks, in this order: suppression (CRM customers, people inside live deals, partners, anyone your sales team is already talking to), prior outreach, ICP fit, name clean-up, identity. Every person lands in exactly one of three files, and the counts reconcile:

| Decision | Meaning |
|---|---|
| READY | Cleared to contact |
| HOLD | Not cleared yet. Each person carries the **one decision** that would release them (for example "colleague of someone mid-conversation: ask the account owner") |
| REMOVED | Do not contact, with the rule that removed them |

House rules that matter on a warm list:

- **Anyone who ever replied is REMOVED**, permanently, whatever the reply said.
- **Anyone contacted in the last 100 days without replying** stays in HOLD until the cooldown ends.
- **A do-not-contact-list company on a warm list goes to HOLD, not REMOVED,** when your own team did the touching: the owner decides with the account owner.
- **Job movers:** check the current title and company before sending. A person who moved to a company outside your ICP is removed; a person whose registration says one company and LinkedIn another is kept only if the message never names a company.
- **Re-run the suppression check right before launch.** Deals and conversations change in days.

### 3. Two follow-up tracks

| Track | Message idea |
|---|---|
| Attended | "Thank you for attending our webinar on [date]. If there was anything specific you were looking to learn, happy to help." |
| Registered, did not attend | "Sorry we missed you at the webinar. If there was anything specific you were looking to learn, happy to help." Include the recording link |

Keep the first touch an FYI with no pitch. A soft pitch, if any, comes 3 to 5 days later. Use a clean first-name field you supply, not the tool's profile name (enrichment can turn a last name into "PhD").

### 4. LinkedIn sequence (one message, no chasing)

1. Check if already connected. If yes: one message after about 3 hours, then end.
2. If not: a blank connection request (withdrawn after about 25 days if not accepted). If accepted: one message a day later, then end. If not accepted after 7 days: one InMail, then end.
3. Nobody gets both the message and the InMail. No follow-ups.
4. Keep each sender under 15 messages a day.

### 5. Build in draft, QC, then launch

- Create lists and campaigns in **draft**. Turn on the tool's exclusion settings (leads in other campaigns, leads in conversation on other accounts, lead and company blacklists).
- **QC before launch:** every lead present, none extra, no duplicates; every custom field equals the upload file; nobody already connected to the sender; zero hits on the do-not-contact list; every sequence path ends; fallback messages carry no variables. **Plant one negative control** (a person who should be caught) and confirm the QC catches it.
- **Read the sequence back** from the tool after building it.
- Tool trap: a settings update can silently reset every flag it does not resend (for example turning the blacklist exclusions off). Resend all flags, then read them back. Some create fields are ignored under one name and work under another; read back rather than trust the response.
- The user starts the campaign in the tool's UI.

### 6. Content that outlives the hour

- An **ungated** recap page with the recording and one downloadable asset (no form: you want reach, and visitor identification can tell you who came).
- Short clips (around 5 minutes each) on the sharpest moments.
- A 3-page written summary drafted from the transcript, reviewed by the speakers.
- A 1 to 2 minute video from that summary, and a blog post under a speaker's name.
- Paid social to a tight audience once the assets are good.
- Name an owner and a date for each asset; post-webinar content is where good intentions stall.

## Recurring community events

For a monthly session: a fixed slot (for example the third Wednesday, same time), a fixed agenda (a 5-minute product update, a 15-minute practical segment, then open Q&A where attendees share their setup for live feedback), and a reason to show up (a giveaway such as a short free pilot for qualifying attendees). Promote through an event page tool such as Luma and the company's own channels.
